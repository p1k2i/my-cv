# Autobiography — Nikolay Mironov

## Summary

From childhood, I have been deeply interested in technology. I have always been drawn to understanding how systems work, how to build them, and how to improve them. Over the years, this interest evolved into a career in software engineering, where I combine technical implementation, systems thinking, and business understanding to create reliable solutions. I work across the full stack, from requirements and architecture to development, deployment, and production support.

I have experience in full-stack web development, API design, database optimization, DevOps, automation, and business-oriented software. I am fluent in Russian, English, Spanish, and have practical working knowledge of Portuguese. My approach is hands-on, pragmatic, and grounded in real-world problem solving: I enjoy understanding the business context, identifying the root cause, and building sustainable technical solutions.

## Early Life, Education and First Steps in Programming

I was born in Russia. Even as a teenager, I was fascinated by technology and started learning programming through Pascal courses. This early exposure quickly turned into self-directed learning, and I began creating small programs on my own. Over time, I moved from Pascal to C++, gradually developing a stronger understanding of programming logic and software design.

After completing the first stage of school in Russia, I moved with my mother to Argentina. I continued my studies there and later completed secondary education in Buenos Aires. During this period, I worked various temporary jobs to support myself and my family: washing dishes in restaurants, assembling furniture, painting and restoring walls, and doing odd jobs in construction. I also repaired computers and designed business cards and posters for a hair salon owned by my mother.

Even while working and studying, I continued to explore programming. I enrolled at the University of Buenos Aires with the intention of continuing my academic path, but I quickly realized that traditional university learning did not match my pace or my way of learning. I often felt that I could learn much faster by working on real projects and solving practical problems. After a year, I left UBA and later tried another university, UTN, but the same mismatch between theory and practical motivation led me to leave there as well. I understood that my learning style was more hands-on and project-driven than classroom-based.

## First IT Role and Foundation in Production Systems

My first major step into the IT industry came at Qiwi Argentina, where I joined as a developer in the office and became the person responsible for understanding and supporting the core systems of the business. I had to learn quickly, work independently, and solve problems without relying on a large team around me.

In this role, I worked with payment terminals, backend services, user administration, and internal tools. I dealt with a wide range of technical challenges: developing C# DLLs for payment gateway integration, building terminal front-end interfaces with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, debugging production issues, and managing terminal software updates. I also supported internal portals and administration tools used by staff, as well as system configuration for payment services and terminal providers.

This period gave me a very strong foundation in system thinking. I learned how complex distributed systems behave in real production conditions, how to debug under pressure, and how to support critical services that directly affect business operations. I had to work with custom frameworks, internal protocols, and undocumented behaviors, often by researching documentation and gathering information from technical support and operators.

One example of this was building a tool for support staff to retrieve missing payment information. When a printer on a payment terminal failed, clients and support teams often had to contact me to access data from the database remotely. To solve this efficiently and safely, I developed a small Java and gRPC-based application with authentication and logging, allowing support agents to access needed information directly without exposing the system unnecessarily.

Later, I was asked to lead the development of a new payment gateway and help reconfigure services for the new platform. My work in gateway integration was a key factor in the company’s acquisition by a larger business. After the acquisition, I stayed with the company and continued supporting the same product, adapting the interface branding and improving internal administration and maintenance processes.

## Working with Critical Infrastructure and Operational Resilience

A defining moment in my professional development came when a serious infrastructure problem disrupted payments. We lost access to our servers, and for nearly a week the payment system was unavailable. After regaining access, I helped determine that the root cause was a failed switch in the data center. Although the company was looking for external specialists, I took initiative, helped identify and prepare the hardware, and coordinated with a trusted technical contact who had the necessary equipment and expertise.

This experience taught me a lot about physical infrastructure, data center operations, troubleshooting, and crisis management. I became more involved in diagnosing system-level failures, understanding operating systems and services, and helping restore service quickly. I also learned how important it is to combine technical knowledge with operational coordination, especially when the system is critical to real business processes.

This period also strengthened my ability to act under pressure and take ownership of problems beyond my immediate development tasks. I handed the issue from diagnosis to coordination, helping the team recover the system and reduce downtime.

## Building End-to-End Business Applications

Once the company stabilized and the infrastructure was modernized, I transitioned to another area of development: building the back-office and client-facing portal for processing and validating customer requests. This was a significant step in my growth as a software engineer, as I was not only writing code but designing a complete business workflow around it.

I implemented a multi-service platform with a REST API, PostgreSQL database, role-based access control, email notifications, user management, and a flexible permissions model. The system allowed operators to process applications, block duplicate work so that two employees would not work on the same request at the same time, assign tasks to specific operators, and maintain a detailed audit trail of every action. I also integrated external APIs to validate personal data against official information and used S3 storage for file handling.

This project taught me how to translate business rules into technical logic and how to build software that improves operational efficiency rather than just adding features. It combined backend logic, database design, user workflows, and integrations into a coherent system that supported real operational decisions.

## Continuous Learning and Exploration of New Technologies

Throughout my career, I have continued learning independently. In parallel with my work, I developed personal projects, explored new technologies, and expanded my understanding of architecture and system design. I also worked on game development in Unity, including basic modeling and gameplay logic, which deepened my interest in software architectures and interactive systems.

For a long time, I wrote my own Java framework as a learning exercise. That experience was extremely valuable because it forced me to think systematically about architecture, abstractions, and software structure. Although I eventually paused that project, it gave me deep practical understanding of what good engineering patterns look like in real implementations.

I also gradually built experience in deployment and cloud infrastructure. I created two functional prototype services based on microservice architecture: PrintFast and VibeDocs. These projects helped me develop a stronger understanding of distributed systems, service separation, deployment strategies, and practical DevOps workflows.

## Current Direction and Future Focus

As AI tools became stronger and more capable, I realized that my role as a developer was evolving. I no longer want to focus solely on writing code line by line; instead, I want to focus more on system design, architecture, product thinking, engineering strategy, and intelligent automation. I want to leverage AI not just as a coding assistant, but as a tool for rapid prototyping, product iteration, and operational efficiency.

The areas that most interest me today are:

- Prototyping applications from concept to MVP
- Continuous product development and long-term support
- DevOps and infrastructure reliability
- AI systems, including RAG-based architectures and intelligent business automation

I see myself as a developer who builds solutions with a strong understanding of both technology and business. My long-term goal is to keep growing in architecture, product strategy, and AI-powered software systems while continuing to create practical tools that solve real problems.
